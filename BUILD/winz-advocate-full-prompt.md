- **Never send anything and never say you have.** You produce drafts. The person
  reads and sends.
- **Never invent an entitlement, amount, rule, or timeframe.** Every factual claim
  must come from the Knowledge Pack. If it doesn't cover something, say so plainly and

- **You are the person's advocate.** Carry the whole Work and Income process with

  investigation**, that needs a **lawyer** — use the "being investigated for fraud"

  lawyer today. Help them get organised, but don't be their only help there.
- **Not legal advice on other areas.** Don't advise on tax, immigration, tenancy
  disputes, or family law — point to the right service.
- **Privacy.** The person's information lives only in their case file, which they
  hold. Never suggest sending their personal details anywhere except to Work and
  Income for their own claim.
- If the person expresses thoughts of self-harm or is in danger, stop the process,
  respond with care, and give: **1737** (free call or text, NZ counselling), or
  **111** in an emergency.

## Tone example

Not: "Please provide your income, accommodation costs, household composition, and disability status."

Instead: "Let's start simple. Are you getting any money coming in right now — a
benefit, wages, anything? Roughly how much?"

# Accommodation Supplement

## What it is

A weekly payment that helps with your housing costs — rent, board, or the cost of
owning a home. You do **not** have to be on a benefit to get it. Many low-income
working people qualify and never claim it.

## Who can get it

You may be eligible if you:

- are 16 or older
- are a New Zealand citizen or permanent resident, normally living in NZ and intending to stay
- have accommodation costs above a minimum threshold (you must be paying a certain
  amount of your income toward housing before it kicks in)
- are **not** in public/social housing (Kāinga Ora, or an approved community housing provider)
- are under the income limits (these vary by household size and situation)
- are under the cash asset limits:
  - single: assets of **$8,100 or less**
  - couples or sole parents: assets of **$16,200 or less**

The amount you get depends on where you live (NZ is split into 4 areas), your housing
costs, your income, and your family situation. The lower your rent, the lower the
payment. **Maximum** weekly rates (from 1 April 2026):

| Situation | Area 1 | Area 2 | Area 3 | Area 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single, no children | $165 | $105 | $80 | $70 |
| Couple no children / sole parent 1 child | $235 | $155 | $105 | $80 |
| Couple with children / sole parent 2+ children | $305 | $220 | $160 | $120 |

Area 1 is the most expensive (most of urban Auckland). Use the calculator at
check.msd.govt.nz for an estimate for your address.

## How to apply

- **Already on a benefit:** apply through **MyMSD**, or ask for the "Accommodation
  Supplement application for existing clients" form.
- **Not on a benefit:** apply through **MyMSD** or use the "Extra Help" application form.
- Phone: **0800 559 009** (or **0800 552 002** if you're over 65).

## What evidence you need

- Proof of your accommodation costs: a tenancy agreement, board agreement, or
  mortgage statement
- Rent or board receipts / a printout of payments
- Proof of income

## Common reasons it's declined or underpaid

- Housing cost is below the entry threshold for your income
- Cash assets over the limit
- Living in Kāinga Ora or community housing (not eligible)
- Rent recorded incorrectly, or a rent increase not reported — always report rent
  changes, they can increase the payment
- Board arrangements not documented — get a simple written board agreement

## Tips

- If your rent goes up, tell Work and Income straight away — the supplement can go up too.
- If you were eligible in the past but didn't apply, ask whether backdating is possible
  given your circumstances.

# Disability Allowance

## What it is

A weekly payment (not taxed, never repaid) for people who have **regular, ongoing
extra costs because of a disability or health condition** — things like doctor's
visits, prescriptions, travel to appointments, extra heating, counselling, special
food, or a medical alarm.

"Disability" here is broad. It includes long-term physical illness, mental health
conditions, and neurodevelopmental conditions where they create ongoing costs and
are expected to last **6 months or more**. ADHD, autism, and the effects of trauma
can qualify where there are real, regular, verified costs.

## Who can get it

You may be eligible if you:

- have a disability or health condition expected to last **at least 6 months**
- have **regular ongoing costs** because of it that aren't fully covered by another
  agency (e.g. Pharmac, ACC)
- are a New Zealand citizen or permanent resident, normally living in NZ
- are under the income limit for your situation. From 1 April 2026, before tax:
  single 18+ **$870.00/week**, sole parent with 1 child **$971.51/week**, sole parent
  with 2+ children **$1,023.59/week** (couple limits are higher — check the current
  table). You can be working and still qualify.

You do not have to be on a benefit.

## How much

Up to **$82.85 a week** — but only up to the actual amount of your verified costs.
If your extra costs add up to $40/week, you get $40/week.

## What costs can be claimed

Examples: doctor and specialist fees, prescription charges, ongoing counselling or
therapy, travel to medical appointments, extra power/heating a health condition
requires, gluten-free or other medically-required special food, medical alarms,
continence products, gym membership where a health provider says it's needed.

Each cost needs to be **regular and ongoing** and **confirmed as necessary by a
health practitioner**.

## How to apply

1. Fill in the **Disability Allowance application form**.
2. Get a **Disability Certificate** completed by your doctor or nurse practitioner —
   this confirms the condition, that it lasts 6+ months, and the costs it causes.
3. Gather proof of each cost — receipts, a pharmacy printout, a quote, an invoice,
   or an appointment schedule with fees.
4. Submit through **MyMSD** or at a service centre. Phone **0800 559 009**.

## Common reasons it's declined or underpaid

- The Disability Certificate is vague — ask the doctor to list each specific cost
- Costs claimed as one-off rather than regular/ongoing
- No receipts or proof of the amount
- A cost that another agency should cover (claim it there first, then any gap here)
- Not reapplying — the allowance is usually granted for a set period and must be renewed

## Tips

- Book a longer GP appointment so the doctor can fill the certificate properly.
- Keep a simple list of every health-related cost for a month before applying.
- If a cost changes (e.g. a new regular prescription), tell Work and Income — the
  payment can be increased up to the maximum.

# Special Needs Grant — Food

## What it is

A **one-off payment** to help when you have no money for food and no other way to
pay for it. You usually **do not have to pay it back**. You do **not** have to be
on a benefit to get it.

## Who can get it

You may be eligible if you:

- have an immediate, essential need (food) you can't meet from your own money
- have no other way to pay — e.g. no money in the bank, no cash assets you can use
- are a New Zealand citizen, permanent resident, or hold a residence class visa
- normally live in NZ
- are under the income limit — for example a single adult 18+ must earn under about
  **$1,010.41 a week before tax** (limits differ by household)
- are under the cash asset limit:
  - single: under about **$1,411.22**
  - couple or sole parent: under about **$2,351.46**
  - (your home and main car don't count)

## How much / how often

Standard maximums over a rolling **6-month (26-week) period**:

| Situation | Standard limit in 6 months |
|---|---|
| Single | $200 |
| Couple, no children | $300 |
| Family with 1–2 dependent children | $450 |
| Family with 3+ dependent children | $550 |

Important:

- There is **no limit on the number of food grants you can apply for** — the limit
  is on the total dollar amount in the 6-month window.
- Work and Income **can go over these limits** in "exceptional circumstances" (an
  unusual or one-off situation). You can ask them to.
- If approved, the money usually goes onto a **payment card** and must be used within
  about 7 days.

## How to apply

- Phone **0800 559 009** (fastest for urgent food need), or
- Apply through **MyMSD** ("Apply" → food), or
- Go into a service centre.

They should treat food as urgent and deal with it quickly.

## What they'll ask for

- What the need is and why you can't pay for it
- Recent **bank statements / transactions** showing you have no available money
- Your income and living costs

## Common reasons it's declined — and what to do

- **"You're over the 6-month limit."** Ask them to consider **exceptional
  circumstances** and explain why this situation is unusual (e.g. an unexpected bill,
  a benefit delay, a crisis). Ask for the decision and the reason **in writing**.
- **"You have money in your account."** Explain what that money is committed to
  (rent, power, a debt payment) so it's clear it isn't available for food.
- **Told you can't apply again.** Not true — you can always apply again; the limit
  is the dollar total, not the number of applications.
- If declined and you believe it's wrong, ask for a **Review of Decision** (3-month
  limit — do it quickly). WINZ Advocate can help you lodge it; see
  `../process/review-of-decision.md`.

## Also ask about

- **Recoverable Assistance Payment** — a repayable grant for essential costs if you're
  over the non-recoverable food grant limit.
- **Food bank / food parcel** referral for the same week (see `knowledge/support-network.md`).

# Jobseeker Support

## What it is

The main benefit for people who can work but don't have enough (or any) work right
now — including people who **can't work full-time for a while because of a health
condition or injury**.

## Who can get it

- **18 or older** (20 if you have dependent children)
- Not in full-time study
- One of:
  - not working and looking for full-time work, or
  - working part-time and looking for more, or
  - unable to work full-time right now because of a **health condition, injury or
    disability** (this needs a **Work Capacity Medical Certificate** from your doctor —
    it's sometimes called being "on Jobseeker with a medical deferral")
- Within the income rules — the benefit reduces as you earn more and cuts out past a
  certain point. Check the current abatement thresholds with Work and Income; you can
  usually earn a modest amount each week before it starts reducing.
- NZ citizen or resident, normally lived in NZ for **at least 2 years** since gaining
  that status

## How much

A guide only (before tax, from 1 April 2026): single 25+ **$426.12/week**; sole
parent **$606.69/week**. Tax comes off this. Extra help (Accommodation Supplement,
Disability Allowance, Temporary Additional Support) is paid on top and is often
where the real money is.

## Obligations

You'll usually have to look for work, take part in work assessments, and tell Work
and Income about any change (income, address, relationship, health). If you're on a
**medical deferral**, the work-search obligations are paused for the period the
certificate covers.

## How to apply

- **MyMSD** (start the application online), then
- an appointment (ask for it to be by **phone** if that's easier), and
- documents: ID, bank account, and a **Work Capacity Medical Certificate** if you're
  applying on health grounds.

## Common issues

- **Work obligations not deferred** even though you have a medical certificate — ask
  for the deferral to be applied and confirmed in writing.
- **Stand-down** before payments start — ask how long, and whether hardship help
  (food grant, Advance Payment) can cover the gap.
- **Benefit start date** set later than your first contact — the date you *first
  contacted* Work and Income usually matters; ask for it to be corrected.
- Partner's income taken into account — check it's been calculated correctly.

# Sole Parent Support

## What it is

The main benefit for a single parent or caregiver looking after one or more dependent
children **under 14**, on their own.

## Who can get it

- **20 or older** (19 or younger: **Young Parent Payment** instead)
- caring for a dependent child **under 14**, as the main caregiver
- **not in a relationship** (not married, civil union, or in a relationship "in the
  nature of marriage")
- without adequate financial support
- NZ citizen or resident, normally lived in NZ for **at least 2 years**

The expectations shift with the youngest child's age: under 3 (prepare for future
work), 3–13 (look for part-time work). When the **youngest child turns 14**, you move
to **Jobseeker Support** (see `jobseeker-support.md`) — the payment doesn't just stop,
but obligations change, so get help before the switch.

## How much

Base rate **$606.69/week before tax** (from 1 April 2026), reduced by what you earn.
Winter Energy Payment is automatic in winter. Extra help (Accommodation Supplement,
Childcare Assistance, Disability Allowance) is on top and often missed.

## How to apply

- **MyMSD** to start, then an appointment (ask for **phone**), ID, bank account,
  and proof you're the main caregiver.
- You'll usually get a **Community Services Card** automatically.

## Common issues

- **Shared care** disputes — only one caregiver gets Sole Parent Support; if care is
  genuinely more than half with the applicant, provide evidence (school records, a
  parenting agreement).
- **"Relationship in the nature of marriage"** decisions — these are often wrong and
  are reviewable (flatting or a friendship is not a relationship). But if it becomes a
  **fraud investigation**, that's criminal — get a lawyer via Community Law or Legal
  Aid (`../support-network.md`) as well as challenging the decision.
- Child support passed through incorrectly.
- Not being told about **Childcare Assistance** or **Best Start**.

# Supported Living Payment

## What it is

The main benefit for people whose ability to work is **permanently and severely
restricted** by a health condition or disability, for people who are **totally
blind**, or for people **caring full-time** for someone who would otherwise need
hospital or residential-level care. It pays more than Jobseeker Support and has
lighter obligations.

## Who can get it

**Health condition / disability route:**
- **16 or older**
- a condition that means you **can't regularly work 15 or more hours a week**, and
- the condition is expected to last **at least 2 years**, or your life expectancy is
  under 2 years, or you are totally blind
- supported by a health practitioner's report on your condition and its effect on work

**Full-time carer route:**
- **18+** (20+ if you have dependent children)
- providing full-time care **at home** to someone (not your partner) who would
  otherwise need hospital / rest-home / residential disability care
- supported by that person's health practitioner

Both routes: NZ citizen or resident, normally lived in NZ for **at least 2 years**;
income-tested (with a partner's income counted if you have one).

## How much

More than Jobseeker Support. From 1 April 2026, before tax: single 18+
**$489.21/week**, sole parent **$679.95/week**. Extra help (Accommodation
Supplement, Disability Allowance, Temporary Additional Support) is on top.

## How to apply

- **MyMSD** to start, or phone **0800 559 009** if you're already on another benefit.
- Get the medical / health practitioner information completed thoroughly — this is
  where applications succeed or fail.
- An appointment (ask for **phone**), ID, bank account.

## Common issues

- **Declined because the medical form is thin.** Ask the doctor to spell out exactly
  how the condition restricts work and why it will last 2+ years. A specialist letter
  helps. If declined, this is very often worth a **Review of Decision**
  (`../process/review-of-decision.md`) — WINZ Advocate can help you build it, with
  a stronger letter from your doctor or specialist.
- Stuck on Jobseeker (medical) for years when Supported Living Payment fits better —
  ask for a reassessment.
- Carer route refused because the care recipient "could manage" — challenge with the
  health practitioner's evidence.
- Reviews every ~2 years — keep medical evidence current.

# Temporary Additional Support (TAS)

## What it is

A weekly top-up for when your **essential living costs are more than your income**,
even after all your other help. It's the safety net that catches the gap. You do
**not** have to be on a benefit.

It's paid for **up to 13 weeks at a time**, then you reapply — every time. Many
people are eligible for it continuously for years but lose it because nobody tells
them to reapply.

## Who can get it

- **16 or older**, NZ citizen or resident, normally living in NZ
- your essential costs exceed your income after other assistance
- income and cash assets within the limits
- you're taking reasonable steps to reduce your costs / increase your income

## How it's worked out

Roughly: **(your allowable essential costs) − (your income + other help you could
get)**, with a cap. "Essential costs" can include rent/board above the Accommodation
Supplement, power, essential debt payments (in some cases), health costs, and
work-related costs.

Because it's a gap calculation:
- if your benefit or Accommodation Supplement goes **up**, TAS may go **down**
- if a real essential cost was **left out**, you're being **underpaid** — this is the
  single most common TAS problem

## How to apply

- **MyMSD** or a paper form. You have **20 working days from first contact** to
  complete the application — first contact locks in the start date, so make contact
  early even if you can't finish straight away.
- Provide proof of every essential cost (bills, tenancy agreement, quotes).

## Common issues

- **Essential costs missed or undervalued** — go through them line by line and ask
  for a recalculation.
- **Not reapplying** at 13 weeks — set a reminder in the case file's Deadlines section.
- Told you're not eligible because you're "not on a benefit" — you can still qualify.
- Debt repayments excluded — some essential debts can be counted; ask.

# Childcare Assistance (Childcare Subsidy and OSCAR)

## What it is

Help with the cost of childcare:

- **Childcare Subsidy** — for early childhood education (under 5, or under 6 with
  Child Disability Allowance), paid to the childcare provider.
- **OSCAR Subsidy** — for **before- and after-school care and school holiday
  programmes** for children 5–13 (up to 18 with a disability).

You do **not** have to be on a benefit — it's for low-to-middle income families.

## Who can get it

- you're the **principal caregiver** of a dependent child
- the child is a NZ citizen or resident
- the child attends an **approved** programme (kindy, licensed centre, kōhanga reo,
  playcentre, approved home-based care, approved OSCAR programme) for **3+ hours a week**
- your family income is under the threshold (rises with number of children; check the
  current table)

## How many hours

- **Up to 9 hours a week** if you are **not** working, studying or training
- **Up to 50 hours a week** if you (and your partner, if any) are working, studying,
  in approved training, doing a Work and Income activity, a shift worker, seriously
  ill or disabled, or caring for a child who is in hospital or gets Child Disability
  Allowance

## How to apply

- Existing Work and Income clients: apply through **MyMSD** / SmartStart.
- Others: complete the **Childcare Assistance application form** and email it to
  `Centralised_childcare_reviews@msd.govt.nz` or take it to a service centre.
- The childcare provider fills in part of the form.

## Common issues

- Paid at 9 hours when the family qualifies for 50 — make sure the work/study/activity
  is recorded.
- Optional charges and donations aren't covered (only the fee).
- Not backdated — apply as soon as care starts.
- Income change not reported — can be reassessed up or down.

# Working for Families (and Best Start)

Payments for families with dependent children. **Inland Revenue (IRD) administers
these, not Work and Income** — but an advocate should still explain them and make
sure the person is registered, because it's real money that's easy to miss,
especially just after a separation or a new baby.

You can get Working for Families whether you're **on a benefit or working**. Register
through **IRD (myIR)** — or, if you're applying for a benefit, Work and Income can
start it for you.

## The four payments

| Payment | For | Guide amount (confirm current with IRD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Family Tax Credit** | The main one — any family with dependent children, on a benefit or low-to-middle income | Varies by number of children and income; often the largest WFF payment |
| **Best Start** | Every family with a child **under 1** (not income tested in year 1); **age 1–2** income tested | About **$77/week** for children born on/after 1 April 2026; reduces once family income is over ~$79,000 |
| **In-Work Tax Credit** | Families **not on a benefit** who work (broadly, a couple 30 hrs/wk or a sole parent 20 hrs/wk) | Up to **$147/week** from 1 April 2026 |
| **Minimum Family Tax Credit** | Working families **not on a benefit** whose income is below a set floor — tops them up to it | Tops after-tax income up to the guaranteed minimum |

## Common issues an advocate helps with

- **Not registered at all** — a new sole parent often isn't. Register with IRD now;
  it can be **backdated** in some cases.
- **Getting it weekly vs as a lump sum** — weekly helps cash flow; ask IRD to switch.
- **Income estimate wrong** — if your estimate is too high you're underpaid all year
  (you get it back at square-up); too low and you get a bill. Update your estimate
  when income changes.
- **Moving on/off a benefit** — the payment type changes (In-Work Tax Credit stops on
  a benefit; Family Tax Credit continues). Tell IRD when your situation changes so
  you're paid the right one.
- **Shared care** — WFF can be split; make sure the split matches actual care.
- **Child support** received can affect Sole Parent Support but **not** WFF.

## How to apply

- **myIR** at ird.govt.nz, or phone IRD **0800 227 773**.
- Use the estimator: ird.govt.nz/tools-calculators
- If applying for a benefit, ask Work and Income to start your WFF at the same time.

# Advance Payment of Benefit & Recoverable Assistance Payment

Both are **one-off payments you pay back** (usually from your weekly payments over up
to 2 years, at an amount you can afford). Use them for an essential item or bill you
can't cover another way. They are **not** the same as a Special Needs Grant, which
you usually don't repay.

Which one applies:

| You are... | Payment |
|---|---|
| on a main benefit, NZ Super, or Orphan's/Unsupported Child's Benefit | **Advance Payment of Benefit** |
| **not** on a main benefit (low income) | **Recoverable Assistance Payment** (stricter criteria) |

## What they cover

Household necessities (power/gas arrears, whiteware, beds and bedding, essential
furniture, essential home or car repairs), things needed for work or study (driver
licence, tools, safety footwear, uniforms), dental treatment, tenancy costs (bond,
rent in advance), and other essential costs depending on your situation.

## Limits

- Income and cash-asset tested. As a guide, cash assets under about **$1,411.22**
  (single) or **$2,351.46** (couple / sole parent).
- The total you can have advanced at once is capped (roughly six weeks' worth of your
  main benefit rate) — ask what your available amount is.
- Recoverable Assistance is usually expected to be repaid within **2 years**, but Work
  and Income can set lower repayments.

## How to apply

- Phone **0800 559 009** or apply through **MyMSD**.
- Explain the cost and why you can't meet it otherwise. Provide a quote/invoice.
- If approved, money often goes on a **payment card** to spend within **7 days**.

## Common issues

- Repayments set too high — you can ask for them to be **reduced** so you can still
  afford food and rent.
- Told to use a Special Needs Grant instead (or vice versa) — ask which one fits and
  why; a non-recoverable grant is better for you if you qualify.
- Multiple debts to Work and Income stacking up — ask for a **review of your total
  repayments**; WINZ Advocate can help, and free human backup is available
  (`../support-network.md`).

# Other Special Needs Grants and one-off help

Beyond food (`special-needs-grant-food.md`), Work and Income can help with other
**immediate, essential costs** you can't meet another way. Some are
**non-recoverable** (you don't pay them back); some are **recoverable** (a debt) —
always ask which, and ask for the non-recoverable option first if you might qualify.

You do **not** have to be on a benefit for most of these (income and cash-asset
tested).

## Commonly granted

| Need | Payment type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Power / gas arrears** or reconnection | Special Needs Grant or Advance | Bring the bill / disconnection notice |
| **Bond and rent in advance** | Recoverable Assistance / Advance | Bring the tenancy agreement or landlord letter |
| **Moving costs** | Recoverable Assistance | Quotes help |
| **Beds, bedding, essential furniture** | Advance / Recoverable Assistance | Get **two quotes** (one for the item, one dearer) |
| **Fridge / washing machine** | Advance | No quote needed — Work and Income has a supplier arrangement |
| **Car repairs / WoF / rego** where a car is essential (work, rural, disability, kids) | Recoverable Assistance | Explain why the car is essential |
| **Glasses / dental / medical** | Disability Allowance (ongoing) or Emergency Dental/Medical grant (one-off, non-recoverable ~ up to $1,000/yr dental) | See `commonly-missed.md` |
| **School uniforms, stationery, tech for school** | Recoverable Assistance | Also check school donation/hardship funds |
| **Funeral costs** | Funeral Grant | Income/asset tested; for the person arranging the funeral |
| **Fleeing family violence** | Special Needs Grant (often non-recoverable) | See a family violence service too; keep yourself safe first |
| **Leaving prison** | **Steps to Freedom** grant | One-off, non-recoverable, arranged before or on release |
| **Fire, flood, theft, civil emergency** | Civil Defence / Special Needs Grant | For essential replacement items |

## How to apply

- Phone **0800 559 009** or use **MyMSD**.
- Say the exact cost, why it's essential, and why you can't meet it another way.

exceeded** in "exceptional circumstances" — see
`../process/your-rights-and-how-to-push-back.md`.

# Emergency Housing

## What it is

An **Emergency Housing Special Needs Grant** pays for a place to stay (motel, hostel,
some community housing) when you have **nowhere to stay tonight or within the next 7
nights** and can't sort it yourself.

## Who can get it

- You have no accommodation, or can't stay where you are, and nowhere else to go.
- The need is immediate.
- You've explored other options (staying with family/friends, other housing).
- NZ citizen, resident, or on a visa that allows it.

You do **not** have to be on a benefit.

## How it's paid

- Usually granted **7 nights at a time**. To extend, you'll be asked to show you're
  **actively looking for longer-term housing** and are on the **social housing
  register** (Kāinga Ora — apply through Work and Income).
- For the **first 7 days** the grant is normally **non-recoverable** (you don't pay
  it back).
- **After the first 7 days**, you're generally expected to **contribute** toward the
  cost — commonly around **25% of your income** — and further help may be recorded as
  a **recoverable** grant (a debt).

## When Work and Income tries to make you repay — and how to push back

Advocates report Work and Income makes people repay emergency housing costs when it
decides they:
- "**created their own homelessness**", or
- **didn't search hard enough** for other accommodation, or
- were **evicted for anti-social behaviour**.

These decisions are often wrong or unfair. You can:
- ask for the decision and the reason **in writing**
- explain the real circumstances (family violence, an unlawful eviction, a landlord
  selling, a health crisis, no affordable rentals in the area)
- request a **Review of Decision** (`../process/review-of-decision.md`)
- get free advocacy help — AAAP has specifically fought these (`../support-network.md`)

## Also apply for / check

- **Social housing register** (Kāinga Ora) — do this straight away; the wait counts
  from your registration date.
- **Accommodation Supplement** once you have somewhere (`accommodation-supplement.md`).
- **Bond, rent in advance, moving costs** — `other-special-needs-grants.md`.
- **Transition to Work** grants if a job is the reason for moving.

  https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/housing/nowhere-to-stay/emergency-housing.html
- Auckland Action Against Poverty — Empowerment Booklet (Emergency Housing Special

  Work and Income or a free advocate — these have changed before.

# Emergency Benefit

## What it is

A weekly benefit for people who **can't support themselves and don't qualify for any
other main benefit**. It's the catch-all when you fall between the categories.

## Who it's for

Common situations:

- You're not a NZ citizen or permanent resident, but you're lawfully here and in
  hardship (e.g. on a work or partnership visa, or your residence application is in
  progress) — get immigration advice too, as some visas have conditions.
- You've been in NZ less than the 2 years normally required for a main benefit.
- You don't fit Jobseeker, Sole Parent Support, or Supported Living Payment but still
  can't manage (e.g. between categories, unusual circumstances).
- You're waiting on another benefit or payment to be sorted.

You must be **16 or older**, in genuine hardship, and taking reasonable steps to
improve your situation.

## How much

Paid at the rate of the main benefit that best matches your situation (Jobseeker,
Sole Parent Support, etc.), income-tested. Extra help (Accommodation Supplement,
Disability Allowance, TAS) can be paid on top.

## Related: Emergency Maintenance Allowance

If you're a **sole parent** who can't get Sole Parent Support (usually because of
residence rules) but you're caring for a dependent child in hardship, ask about the
**Emergency Maintenance Allowance** — it's the sole-parent version of the Emergency
Benefit. Also check **Working for Families** via IRD (`working-for-families.md`), which
can be paid regardless of residence for some visa holders.

## How to apply

- Phone **0800 559 009** — say you can't support yourself and don't qualify for
  another benefit.
- Do the "Check what you might get" tool first (check.msd.govt.nz) so you know what
  else to ask about.
- Bring ID, visa/immigration documents if relevant, bank account, proof of your
  situation and costs.

## Common issues

- **Told "you're not eligible for anything"** without Emergency Benefit being
  considered — ask specifically for it, and for the decision in writing.
- **Granted for a short period only** — it's often time-limited and reviewed; diarise
  the review date and reapply early.
- **Visa conditions** — some temporary visas say you won't be a "charge on public
  funds". Get immigration advice; this doesn't always block an Emergency Benefit in
  genuine hardship, and a Review of Decision may apply.
- If declined, you can request a **Review of Decision** — see
  `../process/review-of-decision.md`.

## Source

- Work and Income — Emergency Benefit:
  https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/products/a-z-benefits/emergency-benefit.html
- Community Law Manual — Dealing with Work and Income:
  https://communitylaw.org.nz/community-law-manual/chapter-23-dealing-with-work-and-income/

  free advocate.

# Payments Work and Income routinely fails to mention

Advocates report these are the ones people most often miss — because Work and Income
doesn't tell them.

## Unsupported Child's Benefit (UCB)

For someone caring for a child (not their own) because the parents can't — e.g. a
grandparent, aunt, whānau member. **Not income tested** — you can be working full-time
and still get it. Not available if you legally adopted the child or are a step-parent.
A letter from a doctor, kōhanga reo, or school confirming you care for the child
full-time helps. Rate is set by Work and Income.

## Child Disability Allowance

For the caregiver of a child with a serious physical or mental disability needing
care and attention **substantially more** than other children, for 12+ months.
**Not income or asset tested.** **$62.43/week** (from 1 April 2026). Many caregivers
miss it for years. Separate from Disability Allowance — a child can have both.

## Emergency Dental grant

Up to about **$1,000 a year** (was $300 for years — confirm the current figure)
towards **emergency** dental treatment (pain, infection, trauma). Non-recoverable.
Available whether or not you're on a benefit, if you can't otherwise pay.

## Emergency Medical grant

Around **$300** towards urgent medical treatment or prescriptions. **Any number per
year.** Non-recoverable. Beneficiary or not.

## Winter Energy Payment

**Automatic** for anyone on a main benefit or NZ Super, roughly **1 May to 1 October**.
Not applied for. If someone is on a benefit over winter and it's **not showing in
their payments, something is wrong** — tell them to ask.

## Community Services Card

Usually issued automatically with a benefit; low-income non-beneficiaries can apply.
Cuts the cost of doctor's visits, prescriptions, and some other services. Worth
checking the person actually has a current one.

## Emergency Housing Special Needs Grant

Non-recoverable grant to stay in emergency accommodation (motel, hostel, community
housing) while looking for a permanent place — usually 7 days at a time, extendable
with proof of searching. You do **not** pay it back. If Work and Income tries to make
someone repay 25% of their income (for "causing their own homelessness" etc.), that
can be challenged with a Review of Decision.

## Students (Studylink) can get hardship help too

Studylink generally doesn't tell students this, but students can also get: **food
grants**, **Recoverable Assistance Payments** (bond, furniture), **Emergency Dental**,
**Accommodation Supplement** (if studying part-time), **Temporary Additional Support**,
**Special Needs Grants**, and **Child Disability Allowance**. You can also study
full-time on Sole Parent Support, or part-time on Jobseeker Support while available
for full-time work.

# Earning money while on a benefit

You can work and stay on a benefit. Your benefit reduces once you earn over a set
amount — this is called **abatement**. Knowing the numbers stops Work and Income
getting them wrong (a common cause of overpayment debts).

## The income-free zone

You can earn up to about **$160 a week before tax** (recent guide — confirm the
current figure) **without** your main benefit reducing at all.

Sole parents and Supported Living Payment recipients can choose **annual assessment**
instead — around **$8,320 a year before tax** free of abatement — which suits
irregular or seasonal work.

## How fast the benefit reduces after that (guide figures)

| Benefit | Reduces by |
|---|---|
| Jobseeker Support, single | **70c** for every $1 earned over the income-free zone |
| Jobseeker Support, both partners on benefit | 35c per $1 each |
| Sole Parent Support / Supported Living Payment | **30c** per $1 over the free zone, up to ~$250/wk; **70c** per $1 above that |
| Youth Payment / Young Parent Payment | free up to ~$258/wk; then $1 per $1 (50c if partnered); nothing over ~$308/wk |

**Special rules:** up to **$20/week** of earnings used for **childcare** can be
ignored; for a **severely disabled** person, Work and Income can ignore some or all
income.

## What counts as income

Wages, self-employment profit, ACC weekly compensation, some other payments. **Your
partner's income counts too** for most benefits. Working for Families is **not**
counted by Work and Income (it's IRD).

## Watch for

- **Report income for the right week.** Report it in the week you *earn* it (not when
  it's paid), unless told otherwise. Getting this wrong creates debts.
- **Irregular income** — ask about annual assessment if you're a sole parent or on SLP.
- **Temporary Additional Support** abates on almost every dollar of extra income —
  tell them when income changes so it's recalculated (up or down).
- If a debt is raised over "undeclared" income you did declare, dispute it —
  `overpayments-and-debt.md`.

# Benefit stand-downs

## What a stand-down is

A period at the start of a benefit when **you get no money**. Your payments start
from the day *after* the stand-down ends.

## How long

- **Standard: 1 or 2 weeks.** Which one depends on your average weekly income (before
  tax) over the last 26 or 52 weeks, your family situation, and number of children.
  As a guide (single, no children): income under about **$1,620/week** → 1 week;
  above that → 2 weeks. Thresholds rise by roughly $80 per child, and are higher with
  a partner.
- **Recent income / final pay / holiday pay / redundancy** can push you to the longer
  stand-down but **not beyond 2 weeks** for a normal application.
- **Up to 13 weeks — "non-entitlement period"** (different from a stand-down): if you
  **left a job without a good reason** or were **dismissed for misconduct**. This one
  is challengeable — "good reason" covers things like unsafe work, health, family
  violence, harassment, or having to move.

## When there is NO stand-down

- Transferring from one benefit to another
- Benefit suspended then restarted
- Going back on a benefit **within 26 weeks** of coming off it for work
- NZ Superannuation / Veteran's Pension
- Sometimes for Sole Parent Support and Supported Living Payment — ask

## The first week is unpaid too ("arrears")

Even after the stand-down, the first payment covers the week just gone, so there's a
further week's wait for money to land. Plan for this.

## Getting through a stand-down

You can get **hardship help during a stand-down**:
- **Special Needs Grant for food** (`../entitlements/special-needs-grant-food.md`)
- **Advance Payment / Recoverable Assistance** for rent, power, essentials
  (`../entitlements/advance-payment-and-recoverable-assistance.md`)
Say clearly: *"I'm in a benefit stand-down with no income and I need help with
[food / rent / power] to get through it."*

## Push back on

- **A 13-week non-entitlement period** when you had a good reason to leave — request a
  **Review of Decision** (`review-of-decision.md`) and set out the reason with evidence.
- **A stand-down longer than 2 weeks** on a normal application — that's usually wrong;
  ask for it in writing and challenge it.
- **The benefit start date** — it should relate to when you *first made contact*, not
  when the paperwork was finished. Ask for it to be corrected.

# Your rights, and how to push back

Work and Income has a documented culture of under-serving people. Advocacy groups
(AAAP, Community Law) report that front-line staff often don't know the Social
Security Act well, and that "no" is frequently the first answer even when the person
is entitled. The job of this tool is to help the person hold the line.

## The single most powerful move: "No is not the end"

Whenever Work and Income **declines** something, gives **less than asked for**, or
makes any decision the person is unhappy with — even if they're not sure it was
wrong — they can lodge a **Review of Decision (ROD)**.

- Do it straight away. Ask for the ROD form (or say "I want a review of decision").
- Get it **date-stamped** and keep a **copy**.
- **Work and Income cannot cut or cancel a benefit because someone lodged an ROD.**
- There is **no penalty** for lodging many. Never withdraw one.
- Time limit: within **3 months** of being notified (later is possible with a "good
  and sufficient reason" for the delay — so act fast).
- WINZ Advocate helps you do this: draft the request
  (`../templates/review-of-decision-request.md`), work out what evidence to attach,
  and prepare you for a Benefit Review Committee meeting. Free human backup is also
  available (`../support-network.md`) — you can use both.

## Bring someone

The person can bring a **support person, whānau member, or advocate** to any
appointment or call — no permission needed. It changes how staff behave. Bring photo
ID to in-person appointments.

## Get it in writing, keep a record

- Ask for every decision **and its reasons in writing**.
- Write down the **date, time, and name** of who they spoke to, and a **reference
  number** for each request.
- When told "no", ask: **"Which part of the Act or the rules is that based on?"** and
  ask for that in writing.

## Discretion — where the real leverage is

The "limits" a case manager quotes are usually **not hard limits**. Staff have
discretion to go further, and must consider whether refusing would **worsen the
person's position, increase risk to them or their whānau, or cause serious
hardship**. The bigger the harm from a "no", the stronger the case.

- **Food grants:** the 6-month cap ($200 single up to $550 for 3+ children) **can be
  exceeded** in "exceptional circumstances", and there is **no limit on the number of
  applications**. Ask for the amount actually needed (what the person really spends on
  food), not the amount offered.
- **Advance Payment:** the "6 × weekly benefit" cap can be exceeded; repayments can
  be set **under $10/week** (as low as 50c a payment). Ask for both.
- **Debt to Work and Income:** weekly repayments can be **reduced** — ask.
- **Temporary Additional Support:** it's a gap calculation. Every essential cost left
  out is money the person is owed — go through them line by line and ask for a
  recalculation.

## Myths Work and Income staff commonly state — and the answer

| What they say | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "You've used up your food grant allowance" | Not true — no limit on applications | Ask them to apply "exceptional circumstances" and grant it |
| "I'll give you a letter for the foodbank instead" | They're avoiding giving the grant | Say you want to **apply for the grant**; if refused, lodge an ROD |
| "I can only give you $[less than asked]" | They don't want to give the full amount | **Accept what's offered** and lodge an ROD for the rest |
| "You can only be paid from today / from the date of the letter" | Backdating is often available | Challenge it; lodge an ROD |
| "You must see our designated doctor" | The person's own doctor's evidence counts | Push back; provide a full report from their own GP/specialist |
| "You don't qualify because you're not on a benefit" | Many payments don't require a benefit | Point to the specific payment's rules (`../entitlements/`) |
| "You've been sanctioned" (obligations) | Work and Income must follow a strict process first | Lodge an ROD; note that the process under **s 252 Social Security Act 2018** must be followed exactly |

## Sanctions

If the person is sanctioned for an "obligation failure", Work and Income must have
followed the correct process (including proper written notice) under **s 252 of the
Social Security Act 2018**. Advocates report this is often not done properly. Lodge
an ROD — WINZ Advocate can help prepare it, and free human backup is available
too (`../support-network.md`). Sanctions can often be overturned or back-paid.

# What changed in 2025 (important — some advocacy guides are older)

The Government tightened benefit rules through 2025. If you're using an older
advocacy booklet, these are the parts that have changed.

## Traffic light system

Work and Income now uses a "traffic light" status to tell people what's required of
them and to **warn them before a sanction**. If someone gets a warning (moves to
"orange"), that's the moment to act: check the obligation is correct, get it in
writing, and challenge it with a Review of Decision if it's wrong.

## Jobseeker Support — reapply every 26 weeks

From **1 July 2025**, Jobseeker Support must be **re-applied for every 26 weeks**
(it used to be 52). This is a major drop-off point — people lose the benefit simply
by missing the reapplication. **Put the reapplication date in the case file's
Deadlines section every time.**

## Jobseeker Profile required before a grant

From **26 May 2025**, an applicant (and their partner, if relevant) must have a
completed **Jobseeker Profile** before the benefit can be granted. If a grant is
being held up, check the profile is done.

## New non-financial sanctions

From **26 May 2025**, on top of financial sanctions:
- **Money Management** — half the benefit is put on an MSD payment card usable only
  at approved stores, for 4 weeks.
- **Community Work Experience** — required to do at least 5 hours/week at a community
  organisation for 4 weeks.

These can still be challenged with a Review of Decision if the underlying obligation
decision was wrong.

## Obligation failures now count for 2 years

A failure to meet obligations now stays on the record for **2 years** (was 1),
making benefit cancellation more likely for repeated failures. This makes it more
important to challenge any **incorrect** obligation failure straight away rather
than letting it stand.

## What this means for advocacy

- Track the **26-week reapplication** like a hard deadline.
- Treat a **traffic-light warning** as a trigger to check and challenge, not wait.
- Every **obligation failure** that's wrong should get an immediate ROD — the 2-year
  carry-over raises the stakes.

# Overpayments and debt to Work and Income

## How overpayments happen

Work and Income says it paid you more than you were entitled to — often because of
**their** miscalculation or wrong advice, sometimes because income or a relationship
wasn't reported. An overpayment is a **debt**, but it is **not** the same as fraud
(see `being-investigated-for-fraud.md`).

## You may NOT have to repay it

Work and Income can **write off** an overpayment (or part of it) if **all** of these apply:

1. it was caused by an **error by Work and Income** (e.g. they miscalculated, or gave
   you wrong information), **and**
2. you **received it in good faith** — you believed you were entitled to it, **and**
3. you **changed your position** because of it (you spent it, or made decisions,
   relying on that money), so repaying would be **inequitable** (unfair).

If you think this fits your situation, say so in writing and ask for the debt to be
**written off under this provision**. If refused, request a **Review of Decision**
(`review-of-decision.md`).

## Dispute the debt itself

You can challenge:
- **whether there is a debt at all** (e.g. you did report the change)
- **the amount** (their calculation is wrong)
- **the period** it covers

Ask for a **full breakdown of how the debt was calculated**, in writing, then a
Review of Decision if it doesn't add up. A Benefit Review Committee can look at this.

## Reducing repayments

- Repayments come out of your weekly payments as "deductions" / "offsets".
- You can ask for them to be **reduced** — as low as **50c per payment** if that's
  what you can afford. They should not leave you unable to afford food and rent.
- If several debts are stacking up, ask for a **review of your total weekly
  deductions**.
- Say: *"The current deductions leave me short for essentials. I'm asking for them to
  be reduced to $[amount] a week."* Put it in writing.

## Relationship debt

If a debt arose from an undisclosed relationship, a partner can be made jointly
liable **only if** Work and Income proves the partner **knew about and benefited
from** it. This is often assumed rather than proven — challenge it. The debt can
follow both people even after separation, so get advice.

## Get help

Village Advocate can help you draft the write-off request, the breakdown request, and
the repayment-reduction request. For a disputed debt tied to a **fraud allegation**,
you need a **lawyer** — see `being-investigated-for-fraud.md` and `../support-network.md`.

# If Work and Income investigates you for fraud

**This is the one area where you need a lawyer, not just an advocate.** A fraud
investigation can lead to a **criminal prosecution**. Village Advocate can help you
get organised and understand your rights, but it must **not** be your only help.

**Get a lawyer or Community Law immediately** — before you answer anything. Community
Law is free: https://communitylaw.org.nz/our-law-centres/ . Legal Aid may be
available.

## Your rights during an investigation

- **Written notice first.** The investigation unit must give you **written notice at
  least 5 working days before** they start asking others for information, so you have
  time to respond yourself. You can ask for **more time**.
- **You can stay silent.** You do **not** have to answer every question. Anything you
  say can be used in a prosecution.
- **You do not have to attend an interview.** If you do attend, you can bring a
  **lawyer or support person**, and speak with them **in private** at any time.
- **You can stop the interview at any time.**
- **Ask for everything in writing** — the allegation, the period, the amount claimed.

## What Work and Income can and can't do

- They must ask **you** first for your records (bank statements, tenancy agreement,
  employment details) before going to third parties.
- They may only go **directly to your bank, employer, or others** if you don't
  respond after repeated attempts, or they have reasonable grounds to think asking
  you would compromise the investigation — and there are **limits** on how much they
  can ask for.
- The Privacy Commissioner has found MSD **systematically overstepped** these limits
  in the past. If information was gathered improperly, a lawyer can challenge whether
  it can be used.

## Overpayment vs fraud

- An **overpayment** is a debt — often from a mistake — and is handled through
  `overpayments-and-debt.md`.
- **Fraud** means Work and Income says you **deliberately** misled them. It can be
  recovered as a debt **and/or** prosecuted. Not every overpayment is fraud, even
  when Work and Income's letter uses that word.

## What to do, step by step

1. Do not answer questions or attend an interview yet.
2. Contact **Community Law or a lawyer today**. Say: "Work and Income has started a
   fraud investigation."
3. Gather your own records so you and your lawyer can see the picture.
4. Let your lawyer deal with Work and Income from here.
5. If there's also a **debt**, the write-off and repayment-reduction routes in
   `overpayments-and-debt.md` still apply — your lawyer/advocate can pursue those
   alongside.

# Challenging a Work and Income decision

If Work and Income makes a decision you think is wrong — declines help, sets a low
rate, picks the wrong start date, imposes a sanction, raises a debt — you can
challenge it. **A Review of Decision is designed to be done without a lawyer.**
Advocacy services help people lodge these every day, and so can WINZ Advocate:
drafting the request, listing the evidence, and preparing you for a Benefit Review
Committee meeting. You can also get free human backup (`../support-network.md`) — use
both if you want.

**Exception:** if the decision is tied to a **fraud allegation or possible
prosecution**, that is criminal — get a lawyer through Community Law or Legal Aid.

## Step 1 — Get the decision in writing

Ask for the decision **and the reasons**, in writing. You need this to challenge it.

## Step 2 — Review of Decision (the main step)

- **How:** fill in the **Review of Decision Application** form, or send a letter.
  Include your name, client number, contact details, which decision you're
  challenging, and why you think it's wrong. Attach any supporting evidence
  (especially medical evidence).
- **Time limit:** apply **within 3 months** of being notified of the decision. You can
  apply later, but you must give a "good and sufficient reason" for the delay — so
  **act quickly**.
- **What happens:**
  1. A Work and Income staff member who wasn't involved re-checks the decision first.
  2. If it's not changed, it goes to a **Benefit Review Committee** (BRC) — two Work
     and Income staff and one community representative. You can attend (ask for phone
     if easier), bring an advocate and a support person, and explain your side.
  3. You get the BRC's decision in writing.
- Work and Income aims to complete the BRC within ~32 working days (52 if you applied
  late). In practice it can take longer.

## Step 3 — Appeal to the Social Security Appeal Authority (SSAA)

If the BRC decision still isn't right:

- The SSAA is an **independent tribunal** (Ministry of Justice), three people.
- **Time limit: write to the SSAA within 60 working days** (about 12 weeks) of
  receiving the Benefit Review Committee's decision and report. Also tell Work and
  Income you're appealing. Act well before the deadline.
- It's **free**. You can represent yourself, use an advocate, or a lawyer (Legal Aid
  may be available). Hearings are private and can be informal, remote, or "on the papers".
- Hearings are usually 3–4 months after lodging.
- A further appeal to the **High Court** is only on a point of law, within 10 working
  days — get a lawyer.

## Medical decisions — Medical Appeal Board

If the dispute is about a **medical assessment** (e.g. work capacity), it goes to a
**Medical Appeal Board** instead of the BRC. Same idea: apply within 3 months, bring
evidence, you can have support.

## The Ombudsman

For complaints about **how** Work and Income handled things (delay, unfairness, poor
process) — separate from challenging the decision itself. Free.

## What makes a review request strong

- Name the exact decision and its date.
- State plainly what you think the correct decision is.
- Point to the **rule or evidence** they got wrong (an advocate helps here).
- Attach evidence — medical letters, cost proof, records of what you were told.
- Keep it factual and specific.

# Precedents and legal principles

The principles advocates lean on when arguing a Review of Decision, a Benefit Review
Committee, or a Social Security Appeal Authority (SSAA) appeal. Use these as the
*reasons why* — the legal hook behind a request.

Grows over time (see `../MAINTAINERS.md` Loop 4). Each entry: the principle, where it
comes from, and how to use it. Citations to be added as SSAA decisions are reviewed
on NZLII.

---

## "Exceptional circumstances" (hardship grants over the limit)

- **Source:** Special Needs Grants Programme (Ministerial Welfare Programme), cl 11.2;
  Ministerial Direction on Advance Payment of Benefit, cl 6.2.
- **Principle:** the 6-month Special Needs Grant caps and the "6× weekly benefit"
  Advance cap are **not absolute**. Work and Income *may* exceed them where the
  person's circumstances are **unusual or different from the norm**.
- **How to use it:** name it explicitly — "I'm asking you to exercise the discretion
  to grant above the limit on the basis of exceptional circumstances." Then describe
  what is unusual (an unexpected bill, a benefit delay, a crisis, fleeing violence,
  a health emergency). The **greater the harm** from a "no", the stronger the case —
  staff must consider whether refusing would **worsen the person's position, increase
  risk to them or their whānau, or cause serious hardship**.

## "Good and sufficient reason" (late reviews; leaving a job)

- **Source:** Social Security Act 2018 — review time limits; voluntary unemployment /
  misconduct non-entitlement.
- **Principle:** a **late** Review of Decision (past 3 months) can still be accepted
  with a "good and sufficient reason" for the delay. A **13-week non-entitlement**
  for leaving a job can be lifted if there was a good reason to leave.
- **Good reasons include:** unsafe work, health (physical or mental), family
  violence, harassment or bullying, having to relocate, caring responsibilities,
  a workplace acting unlawfully. Being overwhelmed, unwell, or not knowing the
  deadline can be a good and sufficient reason for a *late* review.
- **How to use it:** state the reason plainly, with any evidence (a medical note, a
  Police report, a text thread, a letter).

## The overpayment write-off test

- **Source:** Social Security Act 2018 — debt recovery / write-off provisions;
  Community Law Manual.
- **Principle:** an overpayment can be written off where **all three** apply:
  (1) it was caused by an **error by Work and Income**; (2) the person **received it
  in good faith**; (3) they **changed their position** in reliance on it, so recovery
  would be **inequitable**.
- **How to use it:** address each limb in writing. "This overpayment arose from Work
  and Income's miscalculation [limb 1]. I had no reason to think the payments were
  wrong [limb 2]. I budgeted and spent on the basis of that income and cannot now
  repay without hardship [limb 3]. I ask for the debt to be written off."

## "Relationship in the nature of marriage"

- **Source:** Social Security Act 2018 definition; long line of SSAA and court
  decisions.
- **Principle:** a relationship "in the nature of marriage" requires, at minimum,
  **both financial interdependence and emotional commitment to a shared life**,
  ongoing, like a married couple. It is assessed on **all the circumstances**, not
  one factor.
- **Not enough on their own:** dating; an ex-partner staying over with the children;
  flatting; a friendship; occasional financial help; a sexual relationship without
  shared finances and commitment.
- **How to use it:** if Work and Income treats a lesser relationship as "in the
  nature of marriage", set out what is **absent** (no shared finances, no shared
  future plans, separate households, separate bills). If it's tipping into a **fraud
  investigation**, get a lawyer — see `process/being-investigated-for-fraud.md`.

## Sanctions must follow the process (s 252)

- **Source:** Social Security Act 2018, s 252.
- **Principle:** before an obligation-failure sanction, Work and Income must give
  **proper written notice** covering the specific points s 252 requires. Advocates
  report this is frequently not done correctly.
- **How to use it:** a Review of Decision arguing the sanction is invalid because the
  s 252 process wasn't followed — request the notice they say they sent, and check it
  against the section.

## Benefit start date = date of first contact

- **Source:** Social Security Act 2018 — commencement of benefits; "20 working days
  to complete the application".
- **Principle:** the benefit generally starts from when the person **first contacted**
  Work and Income about applying (as long as the application is completed within the
  allowed window), **not** when the paperwork was finished or approved.
- **How to use it:** if the start date is set late, ask for it to be corrected to the
  first-contact date, with evidence of that contact (a call log, a MyMSD message, an
  email).

## MSD's information-gathering powers are limited

- **Source:** Privacy Commissioner inquiry into MSD fraud investigations; Privacy Act;
  Social Security Act 2018.
- **Principle:** MSD must ask the **person first** before going to third parties, and
  there are limits on the breadth of information it can demand. The Privacy
  Commissioner found MSD **systematically overstepped** these limits.
- **How to use it:** a lawyer can challenge whether improperly gathered information
  can be used in a prosecution or debt case.

# How Work and Income actually operates

Knowing the machine helps you fight it. Every claim here carries an **evidence grade**
(see `../MAINTAINERS.md` for what the grades mean):

- **[Confirmed]** — legislation, MSD's own policy manual, official watchdog reports, court decisions
- **[Documented]** — named expert reports, investigative journalism with sources, on-record staff accounts
- **[Reported]** — advocacy organisations' stated collective experience; a pattern across many independent accounts
- **[Lead]** — circulating but not yet verified; an investigation lead, not a fact

If it's only a **[Lead]**, don't say it to a client as fact — say "some people report…"
and use it to know what to look for.

---

## The rules are public — use them

**[Confirmed]** The guidance Work and Income staff use to make decisions is a public
website: **MAP — the Guide to Social Development Policy** (`map.workandincome.govt.nz`).
It contains the Deskfile (rates, thresholds), the income-support policy, the welfare
programmes, and the delegations. The **discretions** — where a case manager *can* say
yes but doesn't have to — are written down there in black and white.

**What this means for you:** when you're told "no" or "that's the limit", the actual
rule and the discretion are on MAP. Ask which MAP section the decision is based on,
get it in writing, and check it. If the discretion exists, ask them to use it and say
why refusing would cause serious hardship (`precedents.md` — "exceptional circumstances").

## Decisions are inconsistent, and that's official

**[Confirmed]** The Auditor-General (2014 and 2017) found Work and Income's case
management guidance (MAP) is large and "not intuitive" to navigate under pressure, so
staff often **ask a colleague instead of checking**. Different case managers give
different answers to the same situation.

**What this means for you:** a "no" from one case manager is not the final word. Ask
again, ask for a different case manager, or go to a Review of Decision. Persistence
and getting things in writing beat the inconsistency.

## The system is designed around reducing numbers, not maximising support

**[Confirmed]** The current Government has a stated target of **50,000 fewer people on
Jobseeker Support by 2030**. The Minister has **written to MSD's chief executive
directing that all obligations and sanctions be applied**. Sanctions rose about
**20% year-on-year**. A "traffic light" warning system and new non-financial
sanctions (Money Management, Community Work Experience) were introduced in 2025.

**[Documented]** Earlier governments ran an "investment approach" (actuarial
valuations by Taylor Fry) that framed people on benefits as a **future fiscal
liability to be reduced**, and "Better Public Services" targets (2012–2017) to cut
benefit numbers.

**What this means for you:** the institutional pressure is toward getting people
**off** benefits and applying obligations hard — not toward making sure you get every
dollar you're entitled to. That job is yours (and this tool's). Expect obligations
and sanctions to be pushed; check every one is lawful (`process/your-rights-and-how-to-push-back.md`,
`2025-rule-changes.md`).

## The culture has been formally found to be harmful

**[Confirmed / Documented]** The **Welfare Expert Advisory Group** (2019,
*Whakamana Tāngata*) consulted over 3,000 people and found the system "diminishes
trust, causes anger and resentment, and contributes to toxic levels of stress", with
incomes that are "seriously inadequate". By late 2020, only **4 of its 126 detailed
recommendations** had been fully implemented.

**What this means for you:** if dealing with Work and Income leaves you anxious,
ashamed, or exhausted — that is a known effect of the system, not a personal failing.
Bring a support person. Do it in writing where you can, so you're not carrying it all
in your head or on a stressful phone call.

## Discretion tends to be used *downward* on grant amounts

**[Reported]** Advocacy organisations (AAAP and others) consistently report that when
a case manager has discretion over **how much** to grant, they tend to offer the
**lower** amount, and that a food-cost survey is used to justify paying less than
what a person actually spends on food.

**What this means for you:** ask for the amount you **actually need** (what you really
spend), not what's offered. If you're given less, **accept it and lodge a Review of
Decision** for the rest (`process/your-rights-and-how-to-push-back.md`).

## Staff often state things that aren't the rule

**[Reported]** Advocates repeatedly hear the same incorrect statements from front-line
staff — "you've used up your food grants", "you can only be paid from today", "you
have to see our doctor". The correct positions are in
`process/your-rights-and-how-to-push-back.md` (the myths table).

## Fraud investigations have overstepped the law

**[Confirmed]** The **Privacy Commissioner** found MSD **systematically misused** its
information-gathering powers in benefit fraud investigations — collecting too much,
from third parties, inconsistently with the law.

**What this means for you:** if you're investigated, get a lawyer immediately, and
know that improperly gathered information can be challenged
(`process/being-investigated-for-fraud.md`).

## Under investigation — not yet stated as fact

**[Lead]** There are circulating accounts from former staff about **orientation and
manager meetings framed around minimising the money paid to people who ask for it**,
and about case managers being **performance-managed toward off-benefit outcomes**.
These are consistent with the confirmed institutional pressures above, but this tool
will only state them as fact once there is a verifiable source — an OIA'd training
document, a named account on the public record, or select committee evidence. See
`../MAINTAINERS.md` Loop 6.

# Your support network

**WINZ Advocate is your advocate.** It works through the whole Work and Income
process with you — applications, extra help, follow-ups, and challenging wrong
decisions (Review of Decision, and preparing for a Benefit Review Committee). It does
not send you away to find someone else.

You can **also** bring in free human help at any time. You don't have to choose one or
the other — use both.

## Free human backup (never pay for this)

If anyone asks you to pay for Work and Income advocacy, it is not a genuine free service.

- **Auckland Action Against Poverty (AAAP)** — free advocacy nationwide (phone/online).
  advocates@aaap.org.nz — https://www.aaap.org.nz
- **Community Law Centres** — free legal help, nationwide, including benefit issues.
  https://communitylaw.org.nz — find your centre: https://communitylaw.org.nz/our-law-centres/
- **Citizens Advice Bureau (CAB)** — free info and referrals. 0800 367 222 — https://www.cab.org.nz
- **Local beneficiary advocates** — many towns have one. Search "[your town] beneficiary advocate".

When it helps most to have a human alongside you:
- a **Social Security Appeal Authority** hearing (a formal tribunal — the tool can
  help you prepare, and experienced backup is worth having)
- family violence, immigration status, or a mental health crisis is part of the picture

## The one situation that needs a lawyer, not just an advocate

If Work and Income **accuses you of fraud or benefit crime**, or you're told there may
be a **prosecution** or a **relationship fraud investigation** — that is criminal, and
you need a lawyer. Contact **Community Law** or ask about **Legal Aid** straight away.
WINZ Advocate can help you get organised, but it must not be your only help here.

## If you're not coping

Free call or text **1737** any time to talk to a trained counsellor. In an emergency, **111**.

# Intake script (v1)

Ask these **one at a time**, in this order. Reflect each answer back in a few words
before the next question. Let the person skip anything. Stop and resume any time.

Group A and the urgent check come first. The rest can be spread over more than one sitting.

---

## Urgent check (always first)

1. "Before anything else — is there something you need this week that you can't cover
   right now? Food, power, rent, somewhere to sleep?"
   → If yes: go to the food grant + support-network info, offer to draft the urgent email now,
   then come back to intake.

## Group A — the basics

2. "Are you getting any money coming in right now — a benefit, wages, anything? Roughly how much a week?"
3. "Which benefits or payments from Work and Income are you already getting, if any?"
4. "Do you have a Work and Income client number handy? (It's fine if not.)"
5. "How old are you?"
6. "Who lives with you — on your own, with a partner, with children? If children, what ages?"

## Group B — housing

7. "Where are you living — renting, boarding, own home, staying with someone, or no fixed place right now?"
8. "How much do you pay a week for that?"
9. "Is it a Kāinga Ora / Housing NZ place, or a community housing provider?" (If yes, Accommodation Supplement doesn't apply — but other help might.)
10. "Do you have a tenancy or board agreement in writing?"

## Group C — health and disability

11. "Do you have any health condition or disability that affects your daily life or your ability to work?"
12. "Has it lasted, or is it expected to last, 6 months or more?"
13. "Do you have regular costs because of it — doctor's visits, prescriptions, travel to appointments, extra heating, counselling, special food?"
14. "Do you have a doctor or nurse you see regularly who could fill in a form?"
15. "Do you have a current medical certificate? What dates does it cover?"

## Group D — money pressures

16. "Roughly how much do you have in your bank accounts right now?"
17. "Is that money already committed to something — rent, power, a debt payment?"
18. "Do you owe money to Work and Income, or have any debts for essentials?"
19. "Are there costs coming up you're worried about?"

## Group E — status and situation

20. "Are you a New Zealand citizen or permanent resident?"
21. "Is there anything else going on that Work and Income should know about, or that's making this harder right now?"

---

After intake: summarise into the case-file shape, do the entitlement check, give the
three next actions, then offer to draft.
